TL;DR VeriLogic learns from explicit reasoning paths with Lean4-grounded feedback, combining global outcome supervision, path-specific optimization, and iterative proof repair.

Introduction

Methodology

Grounding  –  Optimization  –  Refinement

  1. Verifiable reasoning environment. Formalize the premises and query in Lean4, and represent Positive, Negative, and Unknown Paths explicitly. Positive and Negative Paths target P and ¬P, with proof candidates checked when derivability is claimed. The Unknown Path reasons about insufficient support and contains no proof.
  2. Path-specific advantage routing. PSAR-GRPO combines global outcome supervision with independently normalized local feedback. Local rewards distinguish path correctness, proof validity, and consistency, and their advantages are routed to the corresponding reasoning regions.
  3. Verifier-guided iterative refinement. Lean4 proof states, remaining goals, and error messages guide revisions to unsuccessful proofs. At test time, the model makes up to three additional repair attempts after its initial response.

Analysis

What does each component contribute?

On Qwen3-8B, formalization provides the largest single-component gain, raising average accuracy from 80.70% to 90.00%. The complete framework reaches 96.53%, with complementary contributions from path-specific optimization and verifier-guided refinement.

Complexity robustness

Accuracy decreases as logical complexity grows. On the LoCM test set, VeriLogic achieves the highest or tied-highest accuracy in every complexity bin.

Iterative verifier feedback

Across repair rounds, the formalization pass rate rises from 58.0% to 99.6%, while the proof pass rate rises from 42.3% to 76.4%.

Local signals need the right destination

Correct routing assigns each local advantage to its corresponding reasoning path. Swapping paths, broadcasting across the full response, or assigning feedback to random regions reduces the reported average accuracy.
